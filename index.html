I design AI systems that read movement from instrumented everyday objects — not wearables — to characterize motor behavior in children, with a focus on autism spectrum disorder and other neurodevelopmental profiles.

About

I am a PhD candidate working at the intersection of machine learning and pediatric clinical research. My work centers on sensor-based human activity recognition (HAR) applied to instrumented objects — toys, tools and everyday items embedded with lightweight sensing — as a less invasive, more ecologically valid alternative to wearables for studying motricity in young, often non-compliant populations. The long-term goal is to turn object-level movement signals into clinically meaningful markers that support earlier characterization of neurodivergent motor profiles, including autism spectrum disorder (ASD).
